# Ground-Style Seitan Crumbles

This is a 2-cup-broth freezer batch of seitan crumbles meant to be cooked ahead, frozen flat in portions, and browned later like vegan ground beef.

In a large mixing bowl, combine **2 2/3 cups vital wheat gluten**, **2/3 cup nutritional yeast**, **2 2/3 teaspoons onion powder**, **2 2/3 teaspoons garlic powder**, **2 2/3 teaspoons smoked paprika**, **1 1/3 teaspoons cumin or chili powder**, **1 teaspoon black pepper**, and **1/2 teaspoon salt**, if using. Stir the dry ingredients well so the spices are evenly distributed before any liquid is added.

In a separate bowl or large measuring cup, whisk together **2 cups vegetable broth**, **2 tablespoons plus 2 teaspoons soy sauce**, **2 tablespoons plus 2 teaspoons tomato paste, ketchup, or vegan Worcestershire sauce**, and **2 tablespoons plus 2 teaspoons neutral oil or olive oil**.

Pour the wet mixture into the dry mixture and stir until a dough forms. Knead lightly for about 30 to 60 seconds, just until the dough holds together. Do not over-knead. For ground-style seitan, a lighter knead gives a better crumbly texture; heavy kneading makes it more springy and loaf-like.

Let the dough rest for 10 minutes.

Break the dough into rough chunks or flattened pieces. Aim for pieces no thicker than 1 to 1 1/2 inches. Smaller pieces cook more evenly and are easier to crumble later.

## Cooking

Steam the seitan chunks for 35 to 45 minutes, or until firm and cooked through. This gives the best texture for freezer crumbles because the seitan stays firm and does not become waterlogged. If the steamer is small, cook in batches.

Alternatively, preheat the oven to 325°F. Put the chunks in one or more shallow baking dishes with a small splash of extra broth or water in the bottom of each dish. Cover tightly with foil and bake for 60 to 90 minutes, depending on thickness. The seitan is done when it is firm throughout.

## Cooling and Crumbling

Let the cooked seitan cool completely. For the best texture, refrigerate it for a few hours or overnight before crumbling.

Crumble by hand, chop with a knife, or pulse briefly in a food processor. Do not over-process. Irregular pieces brown better than tiny uniform granules.

## Freezing

Portion the cooked crumbles into freezer bags. A good default is **2 cups crumbled seitan per freezer bag**.

Flatten each bag before freezing. This makes the bags stack neatly, thaw quickly, and break apart more easily if only part of a bag is needed.

Label each bag with the contents, amount, and date. Best quality is usually within 2 to 3 months.

## Frying on Demand

Thaw a bag overnight in the refrigerator, or cook directly from frozen if the crumbles were frozen flat and loose enough to break apart.

Heat a skillet over medium heat. Add a little oil, then add the seitan crumbles. Cook until browned at the edges before adding final seasonings.

For taco or burrito bowl crumbles, season with **chili powder**, **cumin**, **smoked paprika**, **garlic powder**, **soy sauce**, and a splash of **salsa or tomato sauce**.

For burger-style crumbles, season with **soy sauce**, **black pepper**, **onion powder**, **garlic powder**, **smoked paprika**, and a tiny bit of **ketchup or mustard**.

For Italian-style crumbles, season with **tomato sauce**, **oregano**, **basil**, **garlic**, **black pepper**, and a pinch of **fennel seed**, if desired.

For packed lunches, brown and season the crumbles before packing. They work well with rice, potatoes, pasta, vegetables, tortillas, salad, or farro.
